If you’ve clicked on this there’s a pretty good chance you’ll be familiar with the concept of xG anyway, so I’ll keep this bit short.

Every time a shot is taken in a football game, a probability is calculated that is associated with it. This represents the probability that that shot would result in a goal. A tap-in from a yard out would have a very high xG, close to 1, and a speculative hit from 35 yards out would have a low xG, close to 0.

Naturally, there are times when the outcome doesn’t go the way the xG would suggest. A team may score from a rare, low quality effort on goal, while their opponents fail to convert any of their more numerous, better chances. You’d expect this sort of thing to even over the course of a season, but have you ever wondered, “What if…?”.

What if a relegation candidate kept scoring from half-chances all season? Could they win the league?

How many goals could be scored in a game in an alternate reality where everyone had their shooting boots on?

Data information
  • Data is taken from Understat.
  • Shots are treated as independent of each other. In real life game-management would come into play, but for a bit of fun such as this we will suspend disbelief.
  • Own goals are modelled in Understat, sparing the blushes of millions of players in this article.
  • League position tie-breaks are done on goal difference, goals for, then a random coin toss.

Imagine if we played the season again. Every shot recreated as if were played again, with the same likelihood it would be scored. Some that were drilled past the keeper would be smashed wide. Others that were skied into row Z would nestle into the top corner. And some would play out exactly the same. How could the league have looked? What if we repeated it again, and again? Maybe our team would fluke an unlikely title! In a million simulations what might happen?
